# Review

## Creative calls

- Storyline A of `STORYLINES.md` ("From a table to a year"): the viewer's own product as it is today (a report), then
  the same twelve numbers as the library's bar chart. B (one number, endless zoom) and C (one stage rebuilt live) were
  not built.
- Every picture of the product is the product: `Chart`, `Calendar` and `WrappedStage` mounted from `packages/*/src`,
  playing their own entrances and the library's own transitions (slash, wave, iris, doors, bloom, fan, spin). The
  numbers are the sample year's (`@react-wrapped/sample`), the chart data the docs' own examples.
- The accent green is kept to one word or mark per shot; the lime only on the logo's dot and the rolling code token.
- The end card points at the product, not the library: "Year in review videos for your product", then the README's
  install line and the docs address.

## What was measured, what was only looked at

- `hyperframes check`: passes. `snapshot --review` beat, seam and phone sheets were read; the fixes they led to are in.
- The render was checked frame by frame at each scene, after a render-only failure was found and fixed (the renderer
  wraps timelines in a proxy without `labels`; `bundle/entry.tsx` reads them from the real timeline).
- The sound was measured, not heard: every effect is placed so its measured peak lands one frame after its picture.
  The render measures -19.9 LUFS integrated, true peak -4.3 dBTP, no clipping: quieter than the -16 to -14 LUFS a
  social feed plays at, which is right for effects alone and wants revisiting once a music bed is in.

## Warnings kept on purpose

- `line_unread` and `contrast_aa_failure` inside the playing stages (the share phones, the engine's biggest day): that
  text belongs to the library's videos passing by, which the film shows as footage, not as copy to read.
- `text_below_floor` on the wall: 21 charts at a quarter of their size are a picture of how many there are.

## Weakest shot and what more time would buy

- No music. HyperFrames' music catalog needs a free HeyGen sign-in (`npx hyperframes auth login`); with it, a bed
  that opens on the colour flood (7 s) and hits on the mark (40.6 s) would lift the whole film more than any frame.
- The engine shot's third scene (biggest day) is on screen for under 2 s. A longer cut, or two scenes instead of
  three, would let it land.
